# Physical UI Principles

The interface must feel like an object, not a collection of div blocks.

## 1. Depth

- Every important layer has depth.
- Cards should feel elevated, separated, and weighted.
- Hero, CTA, input, and result card must feel like distinct physical surfaces.

## 2. Pressability

- Buttons must look pressable.
- Hover means readiness.
- Active means compression, downward movement, reduced height, and contact shadow.
- CTA should feel desirable to press.

## 3. Inertia

- Nothing teleports.
- Reveals, transitions, and panel shifts need micro-resistance.
- Entry motion should suggest mass, not generic fade.

## 4. Materiality

- Surfaces should feel like material.
- One screen should have one dominant material family and one accent material.
- Candidate material cues:
  - soft matte surface
  - warm composite panel
  - glass only if subtle and justified
  - dense tactile control surface

## 5. Tactile Light

- Light defines shape.
- Use edge highlights, ambient shadow, and contact shadow together.
- Avoid flat cards with one weak shadow value.

## 6. Friction

- Not every response should be instant-flat.
- Tiny physical delay can increase perceived quality.
- Use carefully so it never feels slow.

## 7. Elastic Response

- Toggle, slider, tab, chip, and accordion should have slight elasticity.
- Small rebound is allowed.
- Cartoon behavior is not allowed.

## 8. Premium Restraint

- Only 1-2 wow moments per screen.
- Most of the UI should remain calm and expensive.
- Motion and depth should feel intentional, not decorative.

## 9. Visual Mass

- Not all objects should have equal weight.
- CTA, estimate result, and AI message entry should feel heavier than secondary items.

## 10. Physical Hierarchy

- Background is atmosphere.
- Surfaces are planes.
- Components are objects.
- Interactions reveal force and state.
